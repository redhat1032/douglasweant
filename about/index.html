The Saga of Douglas Weant

05 · Longship

The story behind the work. Six chapters, one protective instinct.

ᚹ First watch

Douglas stood watch upon the water, signal lamp in hand. The first task was to notice, understand, and communicate.

What this taught me

Pay attention before you act. A signal only helps if the person receiving it understands what it means.

I start by understanding the system and what it is supposed to do. Then I look at how it works and what could be improved.

ᛗ Service and judgment

From Navy service to Air Force Security Forces – CATM, the work returned to protection, preparation, and judgment.

What this taught me

Protection depends on people and judgment as much as equipment. A procedure has to work when someone is tired, distracted, or under pressure.

That is part of why human behavior matters to me in cybersecurity. A control should help people make sound decisions in real conditions.

ᚱ Systems in industry

In food manufacturing and R&D, the questions became practical: what changed, why did it change, and what happens next?

What this taught me

Changing one part of a process can affect everything around it. An improvement needs evidence from the whole system.

My work in food R&D keeps that practical: question assumptions, try something, measure what happens, and investigate the result.

ᚦ The learning forge

Study opened another workshop: cybersecurity, computer science, and the patient work of turning an idea into something that holds up.

What this taught me

Learning means doing the work, testing your understanding, and staying with a difficult problem long enough to make progress.

I want to co-create with AI, understand what I build, and carry that curiosity into doctoral research.

ᚺ The AI sea

Now the questions reach into AI: how does it shape human judgment, and what happens when an adversary enters the picture?

What this taught me

An answer can look convincing and still be wrong. The interesting work begins when I test it and ask what the evidence supports.

I am interested in how AI changes human judgment, especially when someone is trying to mislead the person or the system.

ᛟ The next horizon

The next horizon is doctoral research and technology that protects people. Different tools, the same reason to stand watch.

What this taught me

The tools change. The reason for using them can stay the same: protect people and make systems better.

My longer-term goal is research that contributes to useful protective technology and a stronger scientific foundation for cybersecurity.

Technology today. Strategy tomorrow. Different era, same game. Old principles, new battlefields. The watch does not end — it changes frequency. From signal lamp to packet trace, from axe to Python, from coffee to code. Defend. Automate. Build. Improve.

04 · Signal Buoys

Six buoys, six interests and experiences. The interactive version is on the home page.

Navy

The first signal was light. Signal lamps, flag hoists, and watchstanding are part of my Navy background. Communication only works when someone is paying attention at the other end.

The connection: notice what matters, communicate clearly, and understand the system before acting.

Food R&D

A process is a system you can test. My day job is hands-on research and development in food manufacturing. It keeps experimentation grounded in what actually happens when a process changes.

The connection: question assumptions, measure results, and follow the effects beyond the part you changed.

WGU

The learning continues. Cybersecurity and computer science are part of my academic path. I want to understand what I build, test my reasoning, and keep working toward doctoral research.

The connection: curiosity needs practice. A useful answer should survive a closer look.

World of Warcraft

Coordination is part of the challenge. World of Warcraft is one of my interests. Coordinating a group puts communication, timing, and adaptation into the same encounter.

The connection: a plan becomes useful when people can act on it together and adjust when conditions change.

Coffee

Fuel for the next question. Coffee belongs here because a personal site should leave room for ordinary interests as well as projects and credentials.

The connection: curiosity is allowed to have a daily ritual.

Food history

How did we get here? Food history connects an everyday subject with a much larger story: people, tools, constraints, and changing habits.

The connection: understanding how something came to be can expose assumptions that are easy to overlook.
