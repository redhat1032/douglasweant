R&D Leadership · Security · Resilience

A practitioner’s path into resilient systems.

My path into technology was not traditional. It has been shaped by leadership in physical security, law enforcement, and industrial manufacturing—fields where failure has real consequences and systems only succeed when people can use them under pressure.

Current Work

R&D leadership in the physical world.

I lead cross-functional work across R&D, operations, and supply chain in large-scale food manufacturing. It is a setting where safety, reliability, regulatory requirements, and real operational constraints are inseparable.

That experience directly informs how I approach cybersecurity and technical architecture: start with the operating environment, understand the dependencies, and design for the conditions that matter when a system is stressed.

The Foundation

Security, systems, and human judgment.

My technical and operational foundation comes from serving as a Navy Signalman and Master-at-Arms and later as an Air Force Security Forces/CATM NCOIC—years spent where policy, pressure, communications, and human judgment collide.

I am completing an MS in Computer Science with a focus on AI and machine learning. My research interests include how people make decisions with incomplete or AI-assisted information, particularly in degraded, adversarial, or high-consequence environments. I am evaluating doctoral study in forensic cyberpsychology as a way to deepen that work.

Outside the Lab

The interests that keep the systems view human.

These are not credentials or a second brand. They are the things that keep me curious about how people, institutions, and technology behave when the plan meets reality.

Post-Apocalyptic Fiction

I read the genre to examine what systems, social structures, and values endure when infrastructure fails. The best stories are rarely about collapse alone; they are about adaptation, trust, and rebuilding.

Gaming

World of Warcraft is a long-running hobby and a useful reminder that complex systems depend on coordination, shared context, leadership, and recovery after a bad decision.

Coffee

A simple constant for long-form technical work, reading, and the kind of conversations that need time rather than speed.

Food History

I research pre-contact South American food systems and the Columbian Exchange: how ingredients, trade, knowledge, and culture moved across the world. It is a living study in adaptation, interdependence, and unintended consequences.
