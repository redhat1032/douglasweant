A personal notebook / A few chapters

Different chapters.
The same curiosity.

I'm Douglas Weant. I like understanding how things work: the technology, the people, and everything that happens when the plan meets reality.

My path runs through military service, food-manufacturing R&D, cybersecurity, and computer science. The setting changes. There is always another question to follow.

01 / Service

Communication. Responsibility. Judgment.

I served in the Navy as a Signalman and Master-at-Arms, then in Air Force Reserve Security Forces. In the Reserves, I was the NCOIC (Noncommissioned Officer in Charge) of Combat Arms Training and Maintenance (CATM) for the 932nd Air Wing and then the 375th Air Wing.

Those roles put communication, security, training, and human judgment into practical terms. A procedure has to make sense to the person using it. A message has to reach the person who needs it. Decisions have consequences beyond the page.

02 / The physical world

Where theory meets the production floor.

I lead cross-functional work across R&D, operations, and supply chain in large-scale food manufacturing. Safety, reliability, regulatory requirements, and operational constraints are part of the same problem.

That work shapes how I approach technical systems, too. Start with the operating environment. Understand what depends on what. Ask what happens when something goes wrong, and whether people can still do what they need to do.

03 / Still learning

One question leads to another.

I completed a B.S. and M.S. in Cybersecurity and Information Assurance at Western Governors University and am finishing an M.S. in Computer Science with a concentration in artificial intelligence and machine learning.

I'm considering doctoral study in forensic cyberpsychology at Capitol Technology University. My research interests include human decision-making, AI-assisted judgment, adversarial environments, and how people and systems behave under stress.

04 / Beyond the workbench

Other rabbit holes.

Some interests start far from a keyboard and lead right back to questions about people, adaptation, and the systems we depend on.

Post-apocalyptic fiction

I'm interested in what survives when infrastructure fails: trust, values, cooperation, and the work of rebuilding.

Gaming

World of Warcraft is a hobby, and a reminder of how much coordination, shared context, and recovery after a bad decision matter.

Coffee

A constant companion for reading, technical work, and conversations that need a little time.

Food history

I research pre-contact South American food systems and the Columbian Exchange: how ingredients, trade, knowledge, and culture move and change one another.
