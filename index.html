01

The Watchstander / Service

Communication. Responsibility. Judgment.

I served in the Navy as a Signalman and Master-at-Arms, then in Air Force Reserve Security Forces. In the Reserves, I was the NCOIC (Noncommissioned Officer in Charge) of Combat Arms Training and Maintenance (CATM) for the 932nd Air Wing and then the 375th Air Wing.

Those roles put communication, security, training, and human judgment into practical terms. A procedure has to make sense to the person using it. A message has to reach the person who needs it. Decisions have consequences beyond the page.

Same ocean. New threats. Higher stakes.

About

A few things about me.

Understand the system. Question the assumptions. Keep learning.

Different chapters. The same curiosity.

I'm Douglas Weant. I like understanding how things work: the technology, the people, and everything that happens when the plan meets reality.

My path runs through military service, food-manufacturing R&D—where real-world constraints have a way of testing every elegant theory—cybersecurity, and computer science. The setting changes. There is always another question to follow.

Earlier chapters included service as a Navy Signalman and Master-at-Arms, and in Air Force Reserve Security Forces as CATM NCOIC. I've since completed undergraduate and graduate study in cybersecurity and am finishing graduate work in computer science.

Those experiences left me interested in how technology, people, and decisions interact—especially when things don't go according to plan.

Outside of work, my curiosity wanders. Post-apocalyptic fiction, gaming, coffee, and the history of food all have a way of leading back to questions about adaptation, cooperation, and the systems we depend on.

This website isn't a pitch deck. It's a place to collect the things I'm learning and the questions I haven't answered yet.
