Douglas Weant is the Director of R&D at Harvest Food Products in Hayward, California.

The path here ran from Navy signal bridges and Master-at-Arms duty, through two decades of food research and development, to graduate study in cybersecurity and artificial intelligence at Western Governors University: a B.S. in 2024, an M.S. in 2025, and an M.S. in Computer Science (AI/ML) one course from done.

This site is the record — the service, the work, the study, and the tools in progress. Everything on it is readable as plain text; the interactive pieces are layered on top.

Signal Buoys

04 · Personal channels

Six buoys, six interests and experiences. Each one connects to the way I think and work.

Navy

The first signal was light. Signal lamps, flag hoists, and watchstanding are part of my Navy background. Communication only works when someone is paying attention at the other end. The connection: notice what matters, communicate clearly, and understand the system before acting.

Food R&D

A process is a system you can test. My day job is hands-on research and development in food manufacturing. It keeps experimentation grounded in what actually happens when a process changes. The connection: question assumptions, measure results, and follow the effects beyond the part you changed.

WGU

The learning continues. Cybersecurity and computer science are part of my academic path. I want to understand what I build, test my reasoning, and keep working toward doctoral research. The connection: curiosity needs practice. A useful answer should survive a closer look.

World of Warcraft

Coordination is part of the challenge. World of Warcraft is one of my interests. Coordinating a group puts communication, timing, and adaptation into the same encounter. The connection: a plan becomes useful when people can act on it together and adjust when conditions change.

Coffee

Fuel for the next question. Coffee belongs here because a personal site should leave room for ordinary interests as well as projects and credentials. The connection: curiosity is allowed to have a daily ritual.

Food history

How did we get here? Food history connects an everyday subject with a much larger story: people, tools, constraints, and changing habits. The connection: understanding how something came to be can expose assumptions that are easy to overlook.

The course so far

1989 — present

  1. 1989 — 1997 · U.S. Navy

    Signalman, then Master-at-Arms

    Visual communications and watchstanding aboard USS O'Bannon (DD-987), then Master-at-Arms at Naval Station Ingleside. A signal only works when someone is paying attention at the other end.

  2. 2000 — 2006 · U.S. Air Force Reserve

    Security Forces — CATM

    Combat Arms Training and Maintenance: NCOIC of CATM for an air wing, and range boss at Beale. Protection takes preparation, instruction, and judgment under real conditions.

  3. 2008 — 2025 · Food industry

    Food R&D and compliance

    Operations and compliance, product development, and regulatory affairs across food manufacturing — including a 900+ product portfolio in grocery retail. A process is a system you can test: change one part, measure everything it touches.

  4. 2024 · Western Governors University

    B.S. Cybersecurity & IA

    The formal turn: a bachelor's in cybersecurity and information assurance, completed in 2024.

  5. 2025 · Western Governors University

    M.S. Cybersecurity & IA

    A master's in cybersecurity and information assurance, completed in 2025.

  6. 2025 — present · Harvest Food Products

    Director of R&D

    Leading research and development at Harvest Food Products in Hayward, California — the day job, and the systems thinking that comes with it.

  7. In progress · Western Governors University

    M.S. Computer Science — AI/ML

    One course from done: the final course ends with the AWS Certified Machine Learning Engineer, Associate exam. A doctorate is the longer horizon after that.
